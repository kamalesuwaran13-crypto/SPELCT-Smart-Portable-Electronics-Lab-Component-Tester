# Week 1 – Product Conceptualization and Requirement Analysis

## Product
**SPELCT – Smart Portable Electronics Lab & Component Tester**

## Objective
Develop a clear, innovative, and feasible electronics hardware product concept by analyzing user needs, current product approaches, technical requirements, feasibility, risks, and the path from concept to production.

## Week 1 Work Completed

### 1. Product Concept
A compact low-voltage electronics learning and component-testing device was selected. It is intended to combine basic component identification and measurement with a guided display interface.

### 2. User Need
The concept addresses the inconvenience of switching between multiple basic tools and reference material during component identification and introductory troubleshooting.

### 3. Target Users
- Electronics/ECE students
- Educational laboratories
- Hobbyists and makers
- Entry-level technicians
- Teachers and trainers

### 4. Core Functions
- Automatic component identification
- Resistance measurement
- Capacitance measurement
- Diode/LED testing
- BJT/MOSFET identification where supported
- Continuity/basic connection testing
- Guided result display
- Rechargeable portable operation
- Optional wireless logging

### 5. Conceptual Architecture
Test Input → Protection & Signal Conditioning → Measurement Front End → ESP32-S3 Controller → Display / Optional Wireless Logging

### 6. Product Development Strategy
The product will progress through discovery, product definition, engineering prototype, verification, productization, and pilot/production-readiness phases.

### 7. Feasibility
The concept is feasible as a staged low-voltage prototype. The first version will intentionally use clearly defined measurement limits. Accuracy, range, protection, calibration, and user experience can then be improved through testing and later revisions.

### 8. Week 1 Outcome
Week 1 establishes the product rationale, target users, preliminary requirements, architecture, development risks, and roadmap needed before detailed circuit design begins.

## Next Stage
Convert the Week 1 requirements into measurable engineering specifications and develop the first safe low-voltage circuit architecture and simulation plan.
